# Airplane Crashes and Fatalities Analysis  
*By Toluwani Emmanuel*  

## Objective  
This project analyzes airplane crashes and fatalities worldwide using Power BI. The dataset covers over 5,000 crashes since 1948, exploring crash patterns, operator categories, aircraft types, and geographic distribution. The goal was to uncover insights about aviation safety over time and highlight lessons learned from history.  

## Tools Used  
- **Power BI** (dashboard design and visualization)  
- **Power Query** (data cleaning and transformation)  
- **DAX** (custom measures and calculations)  

## Data Preparation  
- Dataset sourced from **Data Society**.  
- Contains crash records from 1948 onward, including details such as date, time, location, operator, aircraft type, fatalities, and summaries.  
- Built a unified data model with measures for crashes, fatalities, operators, and aircraft types.  
- Added interactive slicers for **Year**, **Country**, and **Operator Category**.  
- Card visuals display six key metrics at the top of the dashboard.  

## KPIs Displayed  
- Total Crashes: 5,268  
- Total Fatalities: 105,000  
- Total Aboard: 145,000  
- Total Countries: 397  
- Total Operators: 2,470  
- Fatal Crashes: 5,198  

## Key Insights  

1. **Top 5 Aircraft Types Involved in Crashes (Bar Chart)**  
   - Douglas DC‑3: highest crash count (334).  
   - Douglas DC‑4: lowest among top group (40).  
   - Insight: Certain aircraft dominated fleets during specific eras, influencing crash statistics.  

2. **Fatalities by Country (Map + Table)**  
   - Russia: highest fatalities (6,660).  
   - California: lowest fatalities (0).  
   - Insight: Geography and flight corridors play a huge role in aviation risk.  

3. **Top 5 Operators by Crash Count (Bar Chart)**  
   - Aeroflot: highest crash count (179).  
   - Air Taxi: lowest among top group (48).  
   - Insight: Larger fleets face higher exposure to crashes.  

4. **Crash Distribution by Operator Category (Donut Chart)**  
   - Civilian operators: 4,480 crashes (85.3%).  
   - Military operators: 740 crashes (14.6%).  
   - Private operators: 31 crashes (0.6%).  
   - Insight: Most crashes occurred in civilian aviation, reflecting its global scale.  

5. **Crash Fatalities by Year (Line Chart)**  
   - 1972: highest fatalities (2,937).  
   - 1908: lowest fatalities (1).  
   - Insight: Aviation safety has improved dramatically over time, though peaks highlight tragedies that shaped regulations and technology.  

## Outcome  
This dashboard provides a comprehensive view of aviation safety across decades. It highlights how aircraft types, operators, categories, countries, and years reveal different dimensions of aviation history. Beyond numbers, the dataset reflects human lives, lessons learned, and the progress of aviation safety.
