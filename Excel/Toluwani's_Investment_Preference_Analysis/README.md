# Investment Preference Analysis  
*By Toluwani Emmanuel*  

**Tool Used:** Microsoft Excel (PivotTables, Charts, Slicers)  

## Objective  
The goal of this project was to analyze customer investment preferences and understand how people choose where to invest, what factors influence their decisions, and how age and expectations shape their savings objectives.  

## Data Preparation  
- Cleaned dataset to ensure consistency.  
- Structured data into an Excel table for analysis.  
- Added interactive slicers for **Gender**, **Age Group**, and **Expected Return Range**.  

## KPIs Displayed  
- Total Customers  
- Early Adulthood Customers  
- Retirement Planners  
- Customers Expecting 20–30% Returns  
- Education-Focused Customers  

## Key Insights  

1. **Preferred Investment Avenues by Gender**  
   - Mutual funds are the most popular overall.  
   - Equity attracts more males, while fixed deposits are slightly more male-driven.  
   - Provident funds are the least chosen by both groups.  
   - Insight: Men lean more toward higher-risk options, while mutual funds remain widely trusted.  

2. **Investment Factor by Age Group**  
   - Younger customers prioritize returns and risk.  
   - Middle-aged customers focus heavily on returns.  
   - Older customers balance returns and risk but pay little attention to locking period.  
   - Insight: Priorities shift with age — younger groups chase growth, older groups weigh risk more carefully.  

3. **Investment Preferences by Age Group**  
   - Established adulthood has the highest customer count.  
   - Experienced adulthood has the lowest.  
   - Insight: Most of the customer base is in their middle years, a critical stage for financial planning.  

4. **Savings Objectives by Investment Duration**  
   - Retirement planning dominates across most timeframes, especially 3–5 years.  
   - Healthcare appears consistently.  
   - Education is mostly short-term.  
   - Insight: Retirement is the most consistent long-term goal, while healthcare and education are shorter-term priorities.  

5. **Mutual Funds Reasons by Expected Returns**  
   - At lower returns: tax benefits dominate.  
   - At moderate returns: performance and diversification are key.  
   - At higher returns: diversification is the only reason recorded.  
   - Insight: Motivations shift with return expectations — tax efficiency matters at low returns, performance at moderate returns.  

6. **Savings Objectives by Expected Return Range**  
   - Retirement remains the top goal across all ranges.  
   - Healthcare becomes more important at moderate returns.  
   - Education appears only at lower ranges and fades as return expectations rise.  
   - Insight: Retirement is always a priority, but healthcare and education shift depending on return expectations.  

## Outcome  
This dashboard provides a clear view of how customer demographics and expectations influence investment choices. It highlights the role of age, gender, and return expectations in shaping savings objectives, supporting better financial planning and customer engagement strategies.
